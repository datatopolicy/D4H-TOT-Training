Project Principles

  • Outputs published via government platforms (e.g., national public health bulletins) or used to inform policy/decision-making.
  • Government partners identify the questions that need answering.
  • Focus on descriptive analytics using Excel.
  • Introduce health equity — analysis by sex, location, and sociodemographic variables.
  • Government agencies retain data ownership and approve publication.
  • Establish SOPs for institutionalization: templates, data dictionaries, documented data-access processes.

Project Goal

Strengthen the analytical and reporting capacity of government agencies and partners to improve the quality, consistency, and dissemination of public health surveillance and mortality data, including CRVS data when available and relevant.

Why a Training of Trainers Model

This TOT builds a cadre of national trainers who can independently replicate and adapt the workshop model — reducing long-term dependence on external facilitation and sustaining the project's impact after this week.

Project Objectives

  • Train participants to access, clean, analyze, and communicate surveillance and mortality data.
  • Train participants to access and use census data to calculate rates.
  • Establish best practices and document SOPs for accessing surveillance and mortality data.
  • Introduce best practices in data visualization (tables, figures, and maps).
  • Emphasize equity — gender, socioeconomic status, and location — in policy proposals and communication products.
  • Develop templates for reporting on priority diseases and conditions.
  • Establish a monitoring and evaluation framework tracking competency gains, output quality, and post-training data use.
  • Build a cadre of in-country trainers who can independently sustain and scale these practices.

The Seven Project Phases

This TOT is Phase 1 of a seven-phase project. Phases 2–6 follow after this week: government agencies identify topics/teams/data access; participants explore data and set objectives virtually; participants write reports in a 4-day in-person workshop; participants finalize and disseminate reports with mentor support; and participants reproduce regular reports on an ongoing cadence — the true measure of institutionalization.

Pre-TOT Deliverables (due before Monday, March 29)

  • Identify sources of data.
  • For each source, document: department/institution collecting it; timeliness and quality; goals of collecting it; types of reports generated; data dictionaries; process for accessing it; challenges accessing it.
  • A dataset on your priority disease or condition for the past five years, by district and by sex — see Priority_Disease_5yr_District_Sex_Template.xlsx in Materials.

Post-TOT Deliverables

  • Country-specific data analysis project plan.
  • Recommendations for revising workshop materials.
  • Final project report submitted for publication to the public health bulletin.
