# Course Overview

## Project Goal

Strengthen the analytical and reporting capacity of government agencies and partners to improve the quality, consistency, and dissemination of public health surveillance and mortality data — including CRVS (Civil Registration and Vital Statistics) data when available and relevant.

---

## Project Principles

The Data Analysis and Report Writing Project will:

1. **Publish for impact** — Produce outputs published via government platforms (e.g., national public health bulletins) and/or used to inform policy or decision-making.
2. **Be government-led** — Work with government partners to identify the questions that need answering to facilitate decision-making.
3. **Keep tools accessible** — Focus on descriptive analytics using Excel.
4. **Centre equity** — Introduce the concept of health equity and, when appropriate, analyze data by sex, location, and other relevant sociodemographic variables.
5. **Respect data ownership** — Use data collected by government agencies; those agencies maintain ownership of data, approve publication of outputs, and provide the most relevant and recent data available.
6. **Institutionalize** — Establish standard operating procedures to facilitate institutionalization: report templates, data dictionaries, and documented processes for accessing data.

---

## Project Objectives

| # | Objective |
|---|---|
| 1 | Train participants to access, clean, analyze, and communicate surveillance and mortality data |
| 2 | Train participants to access and use census data to calculate rates |
| 3 | Establish best practices and document SOPs for accessing surveillance and mortality data |
| 4 | Introduce best practices in data visualization (tables, figures, and maps) |
| 5 | Emphasize issues of equity — gender, socioeconomic status, and location |
| 6 | Develop templates for reporting on priority diseases and conditions |
| 7 | Establish a monitoring and evaluation framework to track competency gains, output quality, and post-training data use |
| 8 | Build a cadre of in-country trainers/facilitators who can independently sustain and scale these practices |

> **Objective 8 is foundational to this week.** The Training of Trainers model — rather than repeated external facilitation — is what makes the project sustainable after the CDC Foundation's direct involvement ends.

---

## Seven-Phase Project Architecture

```
Phase 1 → Training of Trainers (TOT) ← YOU ARE HERE
Phase 2 → Government agencies identify topics, teams, and data access
Phase 3 → Participants explore data and establish analysis objectives (virtual workshop)
Phase 4 → Participants write their reports (4-day in-person workshop)
Phase 5 → Participants finalize and disseminate their reports
Phase 6 → Participants reproduce regular reports (monthly / quarterly / annual)
Phase 7 → Training of Trainers II — scale-up cycle
```

## Monitoring & Evaluation Framework

Three tiers track project success:

| Tier | Indicator | Collected When |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Competency** | % participants reporting increased self-efficacy (pre/post survey) | Start and end of Phase 3–4 |
| **1 — Competency** | % participants passing the applied skills check | End of Phase 3 virtual workshop |
| **2 — Output quality** | Report rubric score (accuracy, disaggregation, visualization, clarity) | Phase 5 mentor review |
| **3 — Data use** | Report reproduced on intended cadence (Y/N per cycle) | Phase 6 quarterly check |
| **3 — Data use** | Report cited in a policy/planning document (Y/N) | Phase 6 ongoing |
