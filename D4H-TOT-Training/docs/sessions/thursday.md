## Guidelines for Creating Report Templates

**9:30–10:00**

**Standard eight-section report structure:**

1. Executive Summary *(inverted pyramid — finding first)*
2. Background & Objectives
3. Data Sources & Methods *(requires government review before publication)*
4. Results — Findings *(organized by objective, each with a supporting table or chart)*
5. Equity Considerations
6. Discussion — Interpretation & Limitations
7. Recommendations *(clearly separated from findings)*
8. Annexes

**Why the same template for every topic and every future cycle?** It makes the report **reproducible** by someone other than the original team — directly serving the project's SOP/institutionalization principle. A new analyst joining in Phase 6 can produce the next cycle's report without asking the original team a single question.

**Sign-off boundary:** Sections 3 (Data Sources & Methods) and any sensitive district/facility detail typically require government review before publication. The analytic narrative in Section 4 (Findings) is primarily the team's own work, subject to mentor review in Phase 5.

## Drafting Appropriate Recommendations

**11:00–11:30**

**Three distinct sentence types — never blend them:**

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Finding** | What the data shows | "The Eastern district recorded 40% of all reported cases despite having 15% of the region's population." |
| **Interpretation** | What it might mean (tentative) | "This concentration may reflect a genuine local outbreak and/or possible over-reporting linked to a recently upgraded surveillance system." |
| **Recommendation** | What should be done (specific actor + action) | "We recommend the Ministry's surveillance unit cross-verify Eastern district's reporting completeness before allocating outbreak-response resources." |

**A good recommendation:**
- Names a **specific actor** and a **specific action**
- Follows logically from the findings already presented
- Does not introduce new, unsupported claims

> 🗂️ **Facilitator tip:** Run a quick sentence-triage exercise — give participants five example sentences and have them label each Finding/Interpretation/Recommendation. Disagreement usually reveals a blended sentence.

## Training Methodologies (Part 2)

**14:00–14:30**

Building on Tuesday's introduction, this session focuses on the specific coaching techniques trainers will use when they lead Phases 3–4 in their home countries.

**Behavior-specific feedback structure:**

> *"I noticed [specific behavior] / it had the effect of [observable impact] / next time try [specific alternative]."*

Never: "Good job!" or "That was a bit confusing."

**Coaching a struggling co-facilitator live:** Use a subtle clarifying question ("Can you say more about why we use median here?") that gives the co-facilitator a natural opening to recover — without an obvious correction that undermines their standing with participants.

**Recovering from a live technical error:** Narrate the diagnostic process aloud. *"This cell is showing #REF! — that usually means a referenced column was deleted — let me check."* This turns the error into a teaching moment about debugging.
